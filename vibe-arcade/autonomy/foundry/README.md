# PlayJolt Learning Foundry

The Foundry changes the candidate supply, not the QA bar. It selects a reviewed mechanic family and an unused design before any paid provider call.

## Candidate cadence

- odd sequence numbers use the `original` exploration lane;
- even sequence numbers use the `market-benchmark` lane;
- market evidence must come from a fresh reviewed Google Play catalog snapshot;
- benchmarks transfer only abstract product principles such as first-turn clarity, progress readability and feedback cadence;
- names, branding, characters, art, audio, layouts, assets, code and distinctive rulesets may not be copied.

## Learning loop

Each terminal cycle uploads a lightweight `commissioning-summary.json`. `learning.cjs` combines those immutable outcomes with the seed history and records:

- attempts, `RC_READY` and `REJECTED` counts by mechanic family;
- QA pass counts;
- provider calls and estimated cost;
- recurring failure codes;
- used design IDs.

A family with three rejections and no `RC_READY` is retired. A retired family is never given a new game ID to regain its budget. The profile retains at most 24 known failure codes and converts them into at most four correction priorities such as lifecycle integrity, keyboard/touch parity, result presentation, difficulty progression, mobile hierarchy and action feedback; those priorities are injected into the next implementation contract. Blocking input and lifecycle failures receive reserved priority so a large number of later polish failures cannot crowd them out. Unknown text from artifacts is ignored. The selector also excludes used designs, stale benchmark evidence and families or designs without reviewed runners.

Every reviewed Foundry proposal also carries a validated `build_invariants` packet derived from the same immutable multi-seed Oracle used by Product QA. It gives Builder the exact projection, named GameKit action expressions, lifecycle selectors, diagnostic paths, per-stage Oracle complexity checkpoints, preterminal transition and ordinary-input success route for all six seeds. Builder must establish those deterministic core/lifecycle invariants before visual polish. Generated files are rejected before application when they omit reviewed `input.actions` bindings or lifecycle selectors. Replay remains owned by GameKit, and the final action must change the core outcome in the same step. The packet is construction data, not an autoplay or a weaker QA path; Product and Commercial QA still independently exercise the candidate and remain authoritative.

When no eligible reviewed design exists, the Foundry returns `IDLE` before a provider call. New mechanics enter the catalog as `incubating` and require deterministic multi-seed Oracle, Product and Commercial contracts plus a reviewed runner before their status can become `reviewed`.

## Owner taste calibration

After sequence 37, automated construction reliability had improved but owner-visible fun still had no dependable upward trend. The Factory therefore paused paid sequence 38+ intake while the owner compared three zero-provider-call private interaction prototypes at `/lab/taste-calibration/`.

The owner rejected hold-drag-release `Arc Relay` and single-placement `Bloom Circuit` as unfun or unreliable. Continuous radial pointer defense `Halo Guard` is the only viable baseline, contingent on stronger graphics and dynamics. The lab now exposes only its polish surface with phase escalation, motion trails, collision response and combo overdrive. After V2 was judged too difficult, V3 added a visible original guardian character, a wider shield, lower early pressure, four core lives and two forgiving guardian barriers. V4 adds original rescue-wisp and shard-raider characters with readable flight, charge and impact motion. V5 shifted all three original characters to compact chibi proportions and toy-like rendered materials, but owner review correctly identified that those remained flat sprites rather than 3D gameplay. V6 replaces those sprites with local Three.js WebGL meshes, a perspective camera, depth, lighting, shadows and live model rotation while retaining original character design and the easier V4 rules. This still does not qualify an RC or bypass QA.

`taste-calibration.json` is the durable owner-decision record. `SELECTED_FOR_POLISH` records the conditional Halo Guard choice while keeping the effective autonomous daily cap at zero. It can become `SELECTED` only after fresh owner approval of the polished build. After approval the cap is one candidate per UTC day, and two consecutive increases in terminal measured failure count stop selection again with `owner_taste_regression_guard`. Production remains false and all existing Technical, Product and Commercial gates remain authoritative.

The shared finite-state calibration runner used through sequence 28 is retired for later candidates. A new title or theme may not disguise the same choose-values-then-commit loop as a new experiment. Sequence 29 or later stays `IDLE` with `new_interaction_runner_required` until a genuinely different reviewed interaction runner, deterministic Oracle and evidence contracts exist.

Sequence 29 introduced the separately reviewed `arcade-runner.cjs` path and original `comet-catching` family. Owner playtesting then exposed a construction failure: the generated presentation showed descent pressure while the generated core reduced play to hidden lane lookup and immediate teleportation. The visual motion was not the collision authority, and one input could finish each wave. That family remains terminal and is not reused.

Sequence 30 introduced the separate `reflex-runner.cjs` market-benchmark path and `threat-parry` family. Its core is a protected Factory runtime rather than generated candidate code. The same trusted state advances the visible danger value, deadline, parry result, score, progress and terminal outcome. The three waves require two, four and six successful direct parries with two, three and four available directions. Waiting causes a real deadline failure; a wrong direction scores nothing and requires one recovery beat. Candidate generation owns presentation, layout and feedback but cannot replace or duplicate gameplay authority.

Sequence 30 nevertheless ended `REJECTED v6` before browser QA because every provider response hit the same static FeelFX spelling check. That run remains terminal. The validation contract now accepts direct bounded `scene.playjoltFeel` calls with ordinary or optional chaining and returns an exact actionable error; aliases and candidate-owned feedback runtimes remain forbidden.

Sequence 31 is the distinct original `rift-threading` design. Protected `ReflexKit` owns nine closing gates, player lane, opening, countdown/danger, surge collision, score, progress and outcomes. The three stages use 3/4/5 lanes and 2/3/4 gates; left dash unlocks in stage two and right dash in stage three, so the reviewed Oracle measures 2/3/4 consequential choices instead of merely claiming increasing difficulty. Waiting for impact or surging into a wall fails. Only a correct surge scores and advances. This is not a renamed or budget-reset Signal Bastion.

Sequence 31 ended `REJECTED v6` after reaching browser QA. Replay, action feedback, mobile hierarchy and result presentation still failed, so the protected action core did not by itself prove owner-visible quality. Its Technical touch result also exposed a separate QA contradiction: the generic fixed coordinate hit a stage-locked action. Technical QA now derives the pointer probe from the reviewed keyboard-equivalent action binding, and zero-paid preflight rejects any unmapped binding. The terminal candidate remains rejected.

Sequence 32 introduces the separate `weave-runner.cjs` market-benchmark path and `constellation-weaving` family. The reviewed Google Play evidence transfers only immediate manipulation clarity, visible remaining capacity and personal-best replay motivation. It explicitly forbids the benchmark's board, blocks, drag placement, line clearing, shapes, layout, artwork, audio, code and scoring. This is a new direct spatial chain system, not another lane, parry or choose-values-then-commit candidate.

Sequence 32 ended `REJECTED v6` after an exact retrieval-only recovery, with the same immutable game, branch and lifetime budget. Later generations reduced the hard-failure surface and fixed mobile readability and result presentation, but canonical progress, route recovery, normal-input failure, motion, hierarchy, performance and gesture-gated audio remained unproven. The learning artifact is preserved and the design is not retried.

These are construction guarantees, not replacements for Product or Commercial QA.

Product's failure probe was contaminated by a previously advanced reduced-feedback session, and timing-dependent score bonuses let equivalent keyboard and touch routes disagree despite identical protected progression. `ReflexKit v5` removes input-timing bonuses from protected canonical score, and zero-paid preflight now executes every six-seed success route under both immediate and realistically delayed input timing and requires identical projection, score, progress and terminal state. Product failure reachability now starts from a fresh initial browser session.

Sequence 36 introduces the separate `cascade-runner.cjs` market-benchmark path and `pulse-cascading` family. ## Safety boundaries

The existing limits remain unchanged: serial execution, at most six provider calls, at most USD 2 estimated provider cost, at most five repairs and `Production=false`. The Foundry cannot lower QA gates, revive a terminal rejection, reset a repair budget or authorize Production.
